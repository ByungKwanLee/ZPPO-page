Problem
  • Distillation forces a small student to imitate a giant teacher's logits, and it induces memorization, degrading generalization.
  • The natural alternative GRPO is single-model with no slot for a teacher — forcing one in creates an off-policy mismatch and policy drift, degrading generalization again.
  • And even without the teacher, plain GRPO can't recover a learning signal on hard questions: when every student rollout fails, the advantage collapses to zero and the question is silently discarded.
Research Question

How can we transfer the teacher's knowledge to the student without ever imitating the teacher's logits or injecting the teacher's response directly into the student's policy gradient?

Solution

Keep the teacher inside the prompt, never the gradient — BCQ pairs one correct teacher trace with one wrong student trace as anonymised candidates, NCQ confronts the student with all of its own wrong rollouts, and a prompt replay buffer re-exposes each hard question until the student graduates.

method

Three components that compound.

BCQ & NCQ recover a learning signal on hard questions; the prompt replay buffer keeps each hard question in the student's zone until it graduates.

BCQ
Binary Candidate Question. Append one teacher-correct + one student-wrong trace, anonymized & shuffled. Student rolls out again — every gradient-counted token is on-policy.
NCQ
Negative Candidate Question. Append all the student's wrong rollouts at once, with the explicit instruction that none of them is correct.
B
Prompt Replay Buffer. Re-expose each hard question with fresh BCQ/NCQ candidates per visit, until the student graduates (r̄x ≥ 0.5) or is FIFO-evicted.
